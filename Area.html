Everyone should know the formula to calculate the area of a triangle after 5th grade. So this formula should be familiar:1/2*b*h

However, there is definitely more than one way to find the same answer.

Not everyone is familiar with these formulas:

  1. Area=S(S-C)(S-B)(S-C)
  2. Area=1/2ABsin(c)
  3. Area=x1(y2-y3)-x2(y1-y3)+x3(y1-y2)
  4. Area=(S-A)(S-B)(S-C)(S-D)

Formula Explanations

  1. Formula #1 is an accurate way to calculate the area for any triangle with all given side lengths referred to as (A,B, and C). In the formula, the S stands for the semi-perimeter, semi meaning half. The semi-perimeter can be found by (A+B+C)/2. The formula is derived from more simple well known formulas and serious algebraic manipulation. This formula is called "Heron's Formula"
  2. Formula #2 is actually very similar to the simple triangle area formula stating the area is half the base * height. In formula #2, the sine function comes from drawing an altitude in any triangle to make 2 right angle triangles. In a triangle, the height can be represented using the sine function and a side. This formula can be especially helpful when not enough information is given
  3. Formula #3 is an abstract formula used for calculating a triangle's area that is defined on a coordinate plane. To many people, it's hard to find the length of the base and height of a triangle with only 3 pairs of coordinates and no visuals. This formula finds the area of triangles defined by coordinate points with no given information about lengths nor angles.
  4. You might notice Formula #4 is very similar to formula #1. You might think this formula calculates something similar to formula #1 and if you said it calculates a quadrilateral's area, you are correct. However, you might not have known that this formula only works for quadrilaterals that are inscribed inside a circle with all 4 vertices touching the circle. This is because the formula was derived using an inscribed quadrilateral.
