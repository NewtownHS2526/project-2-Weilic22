The function that defines this graph is sin(x)*sin(x^2)

Just take a moment to think about how fascinating calculus is to be able to find the line tangent to any point for this graph. By the chain rule, the derivative equation of the function is

cos(x)sin(x^2)+sin(x)*2xcos(x^2)

By using this equation and substituting a value in the equation. You can find the instantaneous rate of change at any x value.

This graph looks more messy and complex, but there is a way to find the exact area under the curve. NO APPROXIMATING.

An integration method called integration by parts allows us to figure out a formula that can exactly calculate the area under the curve in grid units.

-x^2*cos(2x)/2 + x*sin(2x)/2 + cos(2x)/4

The above expression is the formula used to calculate the area under the curve. How can calculus do that you may wonder. I can't explain it. That's what makes it fascinating!
